# Quantum Workplace: external product research

Research date: 15 October 2026 (Pacific/Auckland). Scope: first-party public product documentation; no access to the organization's tenant, contract, configuration, or usage data. **Identity is provisional:** Quantum Workplace is a strong match for the described employee performance/development SaaS, but the user has not confirmed its URL. The exact phrase “position focus” was not established as Quantum Workplace terminology. Recheck this comparison if another Quantum product is intended.

## Product scope and overlap

Quantum Workplace presents a broad HR platform covering engagement, performance, development, and recognition. Its listed tools include surveys, goals/OKRs, feedback, 1-on-1s, performance reviews, development plans, talent reviews, and succession planning. This is evidence of broad scope, not evidence that every customer must use every module. The vendor says subscriptions depend on organization size and selected tools; actual entitlements require checking the organization's contract. [Platform and pricing FAQ](https://www.quantumworkplace.com/)

Goals cover development, personal, team, and organizational work. The model separates an overall descriptive goal from key results, with an owner, contributors, and followers. Goals can align to larger goals. Consequently, employee goals linked to organizational direction and development are established Quantum capabilities. [Goals: Getting Started](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/goals-getting-started)

The goal creator supports descriptions explaining why a goal matters, custom date ranges, draft goals, restricted or public visibility, and key-result units including **Complete/Incomplete** as well as numeric, percentage, and currency measures. It is inaccurate to claim Quantum requires every meaningful result to be a numerical KPI. The documented creation flow spans name/type, key results, dates, users, and review; whether that feels burdensome for this organization's tasks needs observation. [Create a Goal](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/how-to-use-the-goal-wizard)

Growth offers a career vision, growth areas, plan actions, and manager collaboration. Plans begin private and become available to the direct manager when shared; the documentation also identifies upline managers and relevant admins as permitted viewers. This is a career-development model, which can overlap with but is not automatically identical to a current-position focus document. [Employee Growth guide](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/create-your-growth-plan)

Quantum supports organization-wide competencies and role-based competencies mapped to jobs and levels. Role-based competencies require Growth and can appear in performance reviews according to the employee's role. Thus, role-specific development is not a missing category in Quantum. [Understanding competencies](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/understanding-competencies-in-quantum-workplace)

Managers can inspect team plans, add growth areas and custom actions, comment, and send nudges. The article also describes exports and plan history. Some AI capabilities are described inconsistently as available and coming soon, so their exact availability should be verified before treating them as purchased functionality. [Team Plans](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/team-plans)

## Flexibility and minimal configuration

| Question | Documented evidence | Limit of conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can feature exposure be reduced? | Admins can bulk add/remove permissions for features including Recognition, Goals, Feedback, and 1-on-1s. | This does not prove every navigation element can be hidden or that a minimal configuration is already enabled. |
| Can a team use its own prompts? | Employees/managers can create, reuse, edit, and clone personal 1-on-1 templates. | Available when 1-on-1s are enabled; local permissions and entitlement matter. |
| Can prompts be plain text rather than scores? | Templates support open-ended and bullet-point questions, optional help text, required/optional responses, and optional/disabled comments. | Generic form support does not prove an exact domain workflow or ideal editing experience. |
| Can a template be restricted to a pilot? | Admins can limit template access to individuals or demographics such as department/location. | Organization admins may still be needed to set this up. |
| Must the conversation partner be the line manager? | Admins/current facilitators can change an unfinalized 1-on-1's facilitator; cycle setup supports assigning a specific person. | Do not infer that all Growth permissions or organization relationships can be replaced with arbitrary mentoring relationships. |

Sources, respectively: [User management](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/how-to-manage-users), [User-created templates](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/how-to-create-your-own-1-on-1-template), [Question types](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/1-on-1/performance-review-question-types), [Template access](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/how-to-control-access-to-1-on-1-templates), [Change facilitator](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/how-to-change-the-facilitator-of-a-1-on-1).

Quantum's normal 1-on-1 interface supports agendas, action items, attachments, prior conversations, and a Growth Plan summary. Its documented participant roles are “Employee” and “Facilitator”; managers/direct reports are typical, rather than the sole stated pairing. [1-on-1 overview](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/1-on-1s-checklist), [Using 1-on-1s](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/how-to-use-1-on-1s)

There is also a specifically named Lightweight 1-on-1 feature, documented for organizations without access to full 1-on-1s. It includes recurring meetings, prompts, action items, and finalization. This prevents a blanket claim that Quantum has no lightweight offering; it does not establish suitability for position-focus authoring. [Lightweight 1-on-1s](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/how-to-use-lightweight-1-on-1s)

**Inference:** Narrow scope, a pilot group, custom prompts, nonnumeric completion, or a non-manager facilitator alone are weak claims of product uniqueness. A differentiated experience would need to come from the complete workflow, domain language, defaults, effort required, and sustained user behavior—not merely those individual features.

## Adoption and the organization's non-use

Public documentation cannot diagnose why this organization does not use its subscription. “Too many features,” “poor UX,” and “not flexible enough” remain hypotheses, not findings.

Quantum explicitly recommends piloting with a few teams/departments, explaining the purpose, defining success criteria, collecting feedback, and deciding whether to expand. A minimalist rollout is therefore not inherently novel. [Pilot guidance](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/launching-a-successful-pilot)

Its Growth rollout guide emphasizes manager alignment and participation, employee communication, and structure. Its broader adoption guidance identifies leadership involvement, champions, and communication reminders. These are vendor recommendations, not independent proof of causal effects or evidence about this organization. They nevertheless support investigating rollout and habits alongside software friction. [Growth rollout guide](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/growth-rollout-guide-for-organizations), [Adoption guidance](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/tips-for-successful-tool-adoption)

Growth Cycles add timelines, reminders, and progress tracking, including step-by-step guidance for first-time participants. The July 2026 documentation labels this feature **Beta**. Treat availability as tenant-dependent. [Growth Cycles](https://help.quantumworkplace.com/how-to-use-growth-cycles)

## Evidence to collect before a build-versus-configure decision

1. Confirm the product URL and purchased modules; distinguish subscription ownership from enabled/access-permitted functionality.
2. Ask the system owner what the original intended workflow was, which teams tried it, and where repeat usage stopped.
3. Observe a staff member and facilitator completing the actual position-focus task in the current tenant. Record blocked steps, time, terminology confusion, switching between modules, duplicate entry, and visibility surprises.
4. Configure one minimal 1-on-1 template and a few goals with the existing administrator, using current task language. Test whether a coherent position-focus document can be produced and revisited without substantial workarounds.
5. Compare that configured workflow with Lantern using the same task, participants, and follow-up interval. Distinguish inability to represent the model from merely inconvenient defaults and from absent management habits.

These are proposed validation activities, not claims that a tenant trial has occurred. Public documentation establishes substantial capabilities and configuration options; it does not establish quality of UX, organizational fit, actual adoption, or a full market-wide uniqueness conclusion.
