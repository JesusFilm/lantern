## Question

What must a position focus contain, what existing organizational format or requirements must it follow, and what makes each part useful enough for review?

## Context

The current glossary describes a position focus as an engineer’s stated understanding of their role, current work, intended users and outcomes, reviewed through conversations and able to evolve over time. The parent discussion also identifies expectations, where work comes from and uncertainties as relevant context. These are starting points, not an agreed list of required fields.

No organizational position-focus template was identified in the repository planning files inspected on 13 October 2026. Whether an external template exists remains unknown.

This decision covers content and its quality. Who reviews it and what makes it formally complete are separate decisions. Do not infer that submission, review or approval is the agreed completion criterion.

## Approach

Ask Jaco one question at a time. Resolve content boundaries before dependent details, and use concrete examples to test what counts as a meaningful response. Capture accepted terminology in the glossary when it becomes clear. Keep suggestions and unresolved questions distinct from agreed answers.

## Answer

Resolved with Jaco on 14 October 2026. This is the consolidated content decision; the comments above retain the discussion and superseded proposals.

### Content and purpose

- A position focus should cover role purpose and intended outcomes; responsibilities, current work and its source; users or beneficiaries; expectations and what a good result looks like; and uncertainties or conflicting expectations. These are content areas, not an agreed set of mandatory form fields.
- A critical mission objective is required. It should explain the intended contribution and its connection to the organizational mission: “We partner with the Body of Christ through heart language media, so everyone, everywhere can encounter Jesus.”
- Mission contributions can include product work, shared technical capabilities, and activities supporting team connection or collaboration. Users can be fellow engineers. Social events, demos and show-and-tells are examples, not prescribed assignments.
- A personal-development plan is optional and encouraged. It describes a capability or behaviour to grow and goals showing its practical application.
- SMART goals are required for mission objectives and for personal-development objectives when included. Goal targets and timeframes are to fit the objective; no fixed objective count or numerical targets have been agreed.
- The engineer develops the focus with the career lead and actively revisits it as a reference for pursuing objectives and assessing growth and success.

### Different starting situations

- An engineer with a known direction should connect the users’ needs, intended outcomes and current work. Unknowns should be explicit rather than replaced with invented answers.
- Gaps in understanding are useful starting points for growth and carry an expectation to actively pursue clarification. By the first review, a month after writing the focus, there should be a plan and pursuit of answers should have begun. Resolving every gap by that review is not an agreed requirement.
- Discovery can be an initial mission objective: identify whom the engineer could serve and agree a useful contribution, with SMART goals for what will be learned or agreed and by when.
- If work or direction is absent, leadership is responsible for putting it in place. Jaco, Edmond and Tatai are the responsible leadership group, with Sushma for QA. The engineer can bring possibilities with career-lead support; responsibility for the lack of direction stays with leadership.

### Draft and discussion readiness

- Draft covers preparation in progress and waiting for an agreed mission objective. A draft may need exploratory discussion to settle its direction; draft status does not itself mean the engineer has failed.
- For an engineer with a direction, the agreed minimum for the initial discussion is a mission-aligned objective, SMART goals, and explicit gaps with next steps. Explain users, needs and intended outcomes wherever known. Personal development is optional, with SMART goals when provided.
- Readiness for an initial discussion is distinct from reviewed agreement, formal approval and completion of the first team cycle.

### Reference and remaining decisions

The [supplied working example](../references/position-focus-working-example.png) shows the mission-objective/SMART-goal pairing and a separate development section. Its illustrative counts and month markers are not adopted targets. It has not been established as a mandatory organizational template.

- [Agree position-focus review and completion](02-agree-review-and-completion.md) will settle reviewers, agreement or approval, draft transitions and the formal completion criterion. It also needs to settle the follow-up process and assignment of a primary leadership owner when direction is absent; only the responsible group has been agreed.
- [Agree the guide’s supporting resources](03-agree-guide-supporting-resources.md) will settle the usable guide format, whether any organizational format is mandated, instructions, examples and training resources.

This resolves the content and initial-discussion quality decision. It does not settle the guide milestone, authorize implementation, or fix the provisional January cycle deadline.
