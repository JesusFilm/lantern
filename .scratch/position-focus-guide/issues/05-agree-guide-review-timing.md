## Answer

Resolved with Jaco on 14 October 2026 following his confirmation: “Yes, that captures our agreement and the practical check. Close this decision, update the map, and identify the next step toward specification.” This consolidated answer supersedes earlier timing requirements and proposals; the comments retain discussion history.

- Career leads describe their department’s review process and timing in editable departmental guidance. Departments may follow different approaches; no universal cadence or relationship between reviews is imposed by the application or the guide.
- All review timing, including the first review, is left to that guidance. The fixed first review a month after writing in the content decision is superseded, including for engineering. The annual-period wording in the review-and-completion decision does not establish a required annual schedule.
- Initial drafting and joint confirmation, a mid-year review and adjustment, an end-of-year retrospective and a possible restart are one illustrative approach. Quarterly drafting, review and rewriting are another. Neither example establishes a default or mandatory cycle.
- When timing has not been provided, direct the reader to their career lead. There is no default schedule, required external calendar, or need to await organization-wide calendar dates before specifying this guidance.
- Joint engineer/career-lead agreement and lock-in, the revisable agreed baseline, and explicit gaps with next steps remain unchanged. Removing the fixed interval does not remove the expectation to pursue clarification.
- The agreed practical check is that a reader can distinguish an illustrative cycle from their department’s actual expectations and knows to ask their career lead when timing is missing. Perform this check once the resources exist; it has not yet occurred.
